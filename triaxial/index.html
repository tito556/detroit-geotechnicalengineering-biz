Triaxial Testing in Detroit: Shear Strength Parameters for Foundation Engineering

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

A 15-story mixed-use development along Woodward Avenue required precise effective stress parameters after preliminary boreholes revealed a complex sequence of glacial lacustrine clays overlying fractured limestone. The structural engineer specified consolidated undrained triaxial tests with pore pressure measurement because standard SPT correlations could not capture the anisotropic strength behavior of the Detroit River basin deposits. Our laboratory received undisturbed Shelby tube samples collected at depths between 22 and 38 feet below grade, where the groundwater table sits roughly 7 feet below the pavement surface. The project timeline demanded results within 14 calendar days, and our in-house triaxial frame capacity allowed us to run three specimens simultaneously at confining pressures matched to the foundation stress bulb. Before committing to a deep foundation solution, the design team also reviewed footings options based on preliminary bearing capacity estimates derived from the undrained shear strength envelope we generated from the first batch of specimens.

A single consolidated undrained triaxial test on Detroit glacial clay reveals whether your foundation design is working with 2,500 psf or 4,800 psf of undrained shear strength — the difference between spread footings and a deep foundation.

Process and scope

Detroit sits atop a thick blanket of glacial till, varved clays, and post-glacial alluvium deposited by ancestral Lake Erie and Lake Huron drainage systems. These formations exhibit pronounced overconsolidation in the upper 40 feet due to desiccation and past ice loading, which means triaxial specimens often display a distinct peak strength followed by strain-softening behavior that must be captured through careful strain-rate control and adequate back-pressure saturation. Our triaxial apparatus follows ASTM D4767-11 for consolidated undrained testing with pore pressure measurement, and ASTM D7181-20 for consolidated drained tests on granular interbeds. Each specimen is trimmed to a 2.8-inch diameter and subjected to a saturation phase where Skempton's B parameter must reach at least 0.95 before consolidation begins. We record axial load, axial displacement, cell pressure, and pore water pressure at intervals dense enough to resolve failure within 0.1% strain accuracy. For projects near the Detroit River or the Rouge River floodplain, where soft organic silts appear within 15 feet of grade, we often recommend pairing triaxial data with in-situ permeability measurements to evaluate consolidation timeframes under embankment loads. The complete test report includes Mohr-Coulomb failure envelopes, stress-strain curves, p-q diagrams, and a summary table with both total and effective stress parameters ready for import into PLAXIS or FLAC models.

Local ground factors

Detroit's freeze-thaw cycles penetrate 3 to 4 feet below grade during January and February, causing moisture migration in the vadose zone that can alter the natural water content of shallow clay samples before they reach the extruder. Contractors who postpone subsurface investigation until early spring often face inflated undrained strengths from partially desiccated specimens, which leads to unconservative bearing capacity assumptions when the soil re-saturates after construction. The contrast between summer humidity and winter dryness is particularly pronounced in the clay plains west of Telegraph Road, where seasonal shrinkage cracks can extend 5 feet deep. We mitigate this risk by requiring Shelby tubes to be sealed with microcrystalline wax immediately upon extraction and transported in insulated coolers maintained at in-situ temperature until extrusion occurs inside the humidity-controlled trimming room. The IBC Chapter 18 requirement for site-specific shear strength determination on projects classified as Seismic Design Category C or higher makes triaxial testing mandatory for any Detroit building exceeding three stories, regardless of the foundation type selected.

Other technical services

01

CU Triaxial with Pore Pressure (ASTM D4767)

Three-stage consolidated undrained test on cohesive specimens from Detroit's glacial clay, with continuous pore water pressure monitoring to derive the effective stress friction angle and cohesion intercept. Suitable for short-term stability analysis on excavations and embankments.

02

CD Triaxial on Granular Interbeds (ASTM D7181)

Consolidated drained shear on silty sand and sandy silt lenses commonly found within the Detroit River basin deposits. Provides the drained friction angle for long-term settlement and bearing capacity calculations under permanent structural loads.

03

Multi-Stage Triaxial with Single Specimen

Cost-effective alternative for projects with limited Shelby tube recovery, where a single specimen is sheared in three successive stages at increasing confining pressures to construct a Mohr-Coulomb envelope from one intact sample.

Quick answers

How much does a triaxial test program cost for a Detroit project?

A standard three-specimen consolidated undrained program runs between US$1,960 and US$2,550, depending on the number of confining stages and whether drained shear is also required. This includes specimen trimming, back-pressure saturation, consolidation, shear, and a complete engineering report with Mohr-Coulomb parameters.

How long does it take to get triaxial results from your Detroit lab?

A three-specimen CU program typically requires 10 to 14 working days from sample extrusion to final report delivery. Consolidated drained tests take longer — roughly 18 to 22 working days — because the shear stage must proceed slowly enough to dissipate excess pore pressure throughout the specimen.

Do you need undisturbed samples for triaxial testing, or can remolded specimens work?

Triaxial tests designed to determine in-situ shear strength parameters require undisturbed samples collected with Shelby tubes or piston samplers. Remolded specimens can be used for research or for evaluating fill material behavior, but they will not represent the natural structure or overconsolidation of Detroit's glacial clays.
