MASW and VS30 Testing for Detroit Construction Sites

Together, we solve the challenges of tomorrow.

LEARN MORE →

The soil under a project in Corktown can behave completely differently than a site in Indian Village, even though they are only a few miles apart. Detroit sits on a complex glacial lake plain, where dense clay layers can suddenly transition to loose sands or buried organic silts without surface clues. A standard boring log tells you what is in the borehole. The MASW method tells you how the entire soil column will react during vibration. We run the active multichannel array across the site, collect surface wave dispersion data, and invert it to get a shear wave velocity profile down to 30 meters. The resulting VS30 value feeds directly into the ASCE 7 site class determination, which your structural engineer needs before finalizing the seismic base shear. For deeper stratigraphic control on sites with suspected fill, we often recommend pairing the survey with targeted test pits to ground-truth the near-surface stratigraphy.

A measured VS30 under 180 m/s flips the site to Class E and can double the seismic design forces on the structure.

Process and scope

Detroit's expansion from the 1920s outward filled in former creek beds, buried wetlands, and leveled dunes along the Detroit River. Today, every block carries some remnant of that history. We frequently encounter anthropogenic fill over natural clay, where the fill thickness varies from two feet to twelve feet within the footprint of a single building. The MASW method handles lateral variability well because we can shoot multiple spreads and stitch the 1D profiles into a 2D cross-section. This reveals low-velocity zones that correlate with old stream channels or poorly compacted backfill. When the velocity contrast is sharp, it often signals a buried interface that standard drilling might miss between boreholes. For sites where the client also needs bearing capacity data at depth, the velocity profile can be calibrated with SPT drilling at one or two locations, improving the stiffness correlation for the entire parcel.

Local ground factors

ASCE 7-22 sets the ground rules, but in Detroit the site class penalties can catch projects off guard. Much of the city is underlain by glacial lake clays that plot as Site Class D by default. If the shear wave velocity dips below 180 m/s in the upper 30 meters, the site flips to Class E, and the design spectral accelerations jump significantly. That change alone can add thousands of dollars in structural steel or foundation concrete to a mid-rise building. We have mapped neighborhoods near the Detroit River where soft post-glacial silts push VS30 values into the 160–175 m/s range, right on the threshold. The only way to avoid over-design is to measure, not assume. A MASW line takes a few hours on site and delivers a defensible VS30 profile that the building department will accept without pushback.

Quick answers

How long does a MASW survey take on a typical Detroit lot?

Field work for a single spread usually takes one to two hours, including setup and takedown. We can complete multiple spreads across a standard commercial lot in a half day. The processing and report are typically ready within three business days.

What does a VS30 survey cost for a building permit in Detroit?

The cost ranges from US$1,520 to US$2,720 depending on the number of spreads, array length, and whether we add a 2D cross-section. We provide a fixed-price proposal after reviewing the site plan and understanding the building department requirements.

Can MASW replace borings for site classification?

MASW directly measures shear wave velocity, which is the primary parameter for ASCE 7 site class. The building code accepts it. However, borings are still needed for soil description and foundation design parameters. The best approach combines both, using MASW for the velocity profile and a few borings to calibrate the material properties.

What happens if my Detroit site classifies as Class E?

The structural engineer must use higher seismic coefficients, which usually means stronger lateral systems and larger footings. We can help by refining the VS30 measurement with a closer geophone spacing to see if the site actually meets Class D criteria, or by providing data for a site-specific ground motion analysis under ASCE 7 Chapter 21.
